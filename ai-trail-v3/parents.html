For parents

Your teen already uses AI. This course teaches them to use it well.

AI Trail is a hands-on course for ages 14–17: in-depth lessons on using AI for school and projects, checking what it says, protecting personal data and spotting fakes. No account, no external AI apps.

  • Ages 14–17
  • 4 zones · 10 stations
  • About 20 minutes a station
  • Reading and practice, no videos
  • No sign-up
  • No ads, no trackers

What they learn

1 · Forest Edge

How a chatbot builds its answers, clear prompts, follow-up questions, fact-checking and keeping personal data private.

2 · Study Grove

AI as a tutor that gives hints, not answers. Test prep by having AI quiz them. Honest use in writing.

3 · Makers’ Glade

From idea to project plan, image prompts, their own study bot, and an honest log of what AI did.

4 · Open World

Deepfakes and voice scams (with a family code word), AI bias, AI companions, and how AI changes jobs.

How a lesson works

  1. Warm-up1–3 questions from earlier lessons, spaced out over days so it sticks.
  2. Two partsEvery station has two parts. Each part opens with a guess (“What will the AI do?”), then the real result. Surprise is what makes it memorable.
  3. Read, then practiceA 3–4 minute read with examples and quick self-checks, then hands-on tasks and real prompts with Bloop, the course’s AI helper.
  4. Explain it backThey write what they learned in their own words. Prompts and plans they write are saved to their portfolio.
  5. A natural stopEach lesson ends with a teaser for next time. After 2 stations in a day, the course suggests stopping. A station can be paused and resumed at any task.

How Bloop, the AI helper, is kept safe

Honest about being AI

Every chat opens with “I’m an AI program, not a person.” Bloop is instructed never to act as a friend or partner and to disagree when a teen is wrong instead of just agreeing.

Help for serious topics

Messages about self-harm, suicide or abuse never reach the AI model. Bloop replies with a fixed message: talk to a trusted adult, plus helplines (911/112, 988 in the US, Childline and Samaritans in the UK, findahelpline.com elsewhere).

Clear limits

  • No adult content, violence, weapons, drugs or hacking
  • Swearing is filtered before it reaches the model
  • Asks teens not to share personal details
  • Won’t write graded work for them: hints and feedback instead
  • A few messages per task, not an endless chat

What happens to data

Stored in the browser only

The name or nickname your teen enters, their progress and their portfolio stay in this browser on this device. There is no account and no copy on our side.

Messages to Bloop

When your teen writes to Bloop, the message goes to our assistant server and an AI model on Cloudflare Workers AI writes the reply. We don’t save the conversations. When a safety filter triggers, we log only the type of filter, never the message.

No tracking

No ads, no analytics, no third-party trackers. Even the fonts are hosted on our site, so loading the page doesn’t contact Google.

Your controls

  1. Daily limit with a PINIn the course: Settings → For parents. Choose 1–3 stations a day and set a 4-digit PIN so only you can change it. Resetting progress doesn’t remove the limit.
  2. Time spentThe same panel shows time in lessons today and this week.
  3. A weekly goal, not a streakThe goal is 2 stations a week. Missing a day costs nothing, so there’s no pressure to log in every day, and the course never sends notifications.
  4. See what they madeProfile → Portfolio shows the prompts and plans your teen wrote. Profile → Skills shows what each finished zone covers.

Because everything lives in the browser, a teen could clear the site data to get past the limit, though that also deletes their progress. A firmer control needs accounts, which we haven’t added yet.

Questions parents ask

Does my teen need ChatGPT or another AI account?

No. All practice happens with Bloop inside the course. Most public AI apps require users to be at least 13, and teens usually need a parent’s permission, so check each app’s rules if your teen uses one outside the course.

Won’t this make them rely on AI even more?

The course teaches the opposite habit: try it yourself first, ask AI for hints instead of answers, check facts in real sources, and know when to talk to a person instead.

What about cheating on homework?

A whole zone is about honest use: AI as a tutor and editor, never as the author, and how to say when AI helped.

Is the certificate an official qualification?

No. It lists the skills covered in the course and is generated in the browser when your teen finishes. It is not accredited.

Can Bloop be wrong?

Yes, like any AI. The course says so in every chat, and spotting AI mistakes is one of the first things it teaches.
