# Sources for the kids AI course

Raw sources, not paraphrased yet. Searched 2026-10-20.
Age column = which track the source fits best as raw material (6–8 / 9–11 / 12–14). "Adult" = source is for adults; you adapt it.

## 1. Basics: what AI is and how it works

| Source | Type | Age fit | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Code.org: How AI Works](https://code.org/ai/how-ai-works) | Video series (2–7 min each) + free lesson plans | 12–14 (some 9–11) | Machine learning, neural networks, LLMs, generative AI, ethics. Each video has a lesson plan. Best single source for the course. |
| [Day of AI + Common Sense: "What is AI?"](https://ges.goochlandschools.org/o/ges/article/2466882) | Short family video | 9–11, parents | What AI is, how it works, why it matters, tips for talking to kids. |
| [Kapow Primary: What is AI?](https://www.kapowprimary.com/video/pupil-video-what-is-ai/) | Classroom video | 9–11 | AI = computers doing tasks that need people; machine learning via "learning to spot cats from thousands of photos". Good analogy. |
| [micro:bit: Video "What is AI?"](https://microbit.org/teach/classroom-resources/video-what-is-ai/) | Classroom video | 9–11 | Everyday examples of AI, what it is good and bad at. |
| [Crash Course Artificial Intelligence](https://thecrashcourse.com/courses/crash-course-artificial-intelligence-preview/) | YouTube series, 20 eps × ~12 min (2019) | 12–14 | Supervised learning, neural nets, NLP, bias. Solid but pre-ChatGPT: no chatbots, no prompting. |
| [Google: Be Internet Awesome (educators)](https://beinternetawesome.withgoogle.com/en_us/educators) | Free curriculum, grades 2–8 | 6–8, 9–11 | AI Literacy Guide: machine learning, data, algorithms. No login. Best match for the youngest track. |
| [Google AI literacy resources hub](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/education/google-ai-literacy-tools-2025/) | Article / hub | Parents, 12–14 | Index of Google's AI resources for students, parents, teachers. |
| [Better Internet for Kids (EU): AI, what is it?](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/en/resource-directory/artificial-intelligence-what-it) | Resource directory | 9–11 | EU-curated kid resource on AI basics. |

## 2. Prompting: how to get good answers

| Source | Type | Age fit | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|---|
| [OpenAI: Prompt engineering best practices for ChatGPT](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10032626-prompt-engineering-best-practices-for-chatgpt) | Official article | Adult → 12–14 | Be clear, give context, name the tone, iterate (try → check → fix). Short. |
| [Google: Prompting guide 101 (PDF)](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/gemini-for-google-workspace-prompting-guide-101.pdf) | Official handbook | Adult → 9–14 | Framework: **Persona, Task, Context, Format**. Easy to turn into a kids' "4-part recipe". Examples are work-focused; swap for homework/hobby. |
| [Google Prompting Essentials](https://grow.google/prompting-essentials) | Course + video (~9 h) | Adult | 5-step prompt framework, 4 ways to improve an answer. Mine it for structure. |
| [Anthropic: Prompt engineering overview](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview) | Official docs | Adult | "Be clear and direct" is #1. Key test: if a friend with no context would be confused by your prompt, the AI will be too. Great line for kids. |
| [Anthropic AI Fluency courses on Coursera (incl. "AI Fluency for Students")](https://blog.coursera.org/anthropic-launches-five-free-courses-on-coursera-to-help-build-ai-fluency/) | Free video courses | 12–14, teens | Using AI as a learning partner without losing your own thinking. Fits the "critical thinking" module. |
| [Khan Academy: How to create effective AI prompts for learning](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-to-create-effective-ai-prompts-khanmigo-kl/) | Article | 9–14 | Prompts for learning (simple, clear, with a purpose). Student-oriented. |
| [Jeff Su: "What Makes a Prompt Actually Work?" (via list)](https://glasp.co/posts/10-best-videos-to-learn-prompt-engineering) | YouTube, ~8 min | Adult → 12–14 | 6 parts of a prompt: task, context, examples, persona, format, tone. Link is a list of 10 prompting videos; find his on YouTube. |

## 3. Safety: what NOT to share with AI + AI makes mistakes

| Source | Type | Age fit | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Norton: Don't Feed the Bots: What Not to Share with AI Chatbots](https://us.norton.com/blog/privacy/what-not-to-share-with-chatbots) | Article | Adult → all | Why: chatbots store and analyze what you type. |
| [Bitdefender: What Not to Share With AI Chatbots](https://www.bitdefender.com/blog/hotforsecurity/what-not-to-share-with-ai-chatbots/) | Article | Adult → all | Clear list: full name, address, phone, birthday, ID numbers, passwords/codes, card/bank info, very private feelings, secrets. |
| [The Week: 10 things you should never tell AI chatbots](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2025/12/10/10-things-you-should-never-tell-ai-chatbots-like-chatgpt-gemini.amp.html) | Article (Dec 2025) | Adult | A ready "top 10" list to simplify for kids. |
| [Vodafone + NSPCC: top 10 tips to talk to kids about AI chatbots](https://www.vodafone.co.uk/newscentre/features/nspcc-top-10-tips-parents-how-talk-to-their-children-about-ai-chatbot-use/) | Article (Feb 2026) | Parents → all | Kid-specific: never share full name, address, school, phone, photos. "Treat a chat like talking in a public place." Best kid-safety line found. |
| [Day of AI + Common Sense: "Talking to Kids About AI" toolkit](https://dayofai.org/news/day-of-ai-and-common-sense-media-launch-essential-toolkit-for-parents-and-families-talking-to-kids-about-ai-privacy-fairness-and-responsibility) | Parent toolkit (Nov 2025) | Parents, 9–14 | Privacy, bias, misinformation, keeping human connection. |
| [Common Sense Media: AI hub + product risk ratings](https://www.commonsense.org/ai) | Ratings / guides | Parents | Risk ratings of ChatGPT, Gemini, Meta AI, AI toys. Useful for parent-facing marketing. |
| [Common Sense: Report on Teens, Tweens and AI](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/a-comprehensive-report-on-teens-tweens-and-ai) | Research (2026) | Parents | Stats: 86% of kids 9–17 use AI; 1 in 6 saw inappropriate content; only 1/3 told an adult. Strong hook for selling to parents. |
| [Internet Matters: "Me, Myself and AI" research](https://www.internetmatters.org/hub/research/me-myself-and-ai-chatbot-research/) | Research (UK) | Parents | Kids use chatbots for homework, advice, friendship; parent concerns (over-reliance, accuracy). |
| [Coddy: "The AI Can Be Wrong"](https://coddy.tech/learn/prompts/fundamentals/the_ai_can_be_wrong) | Mini-lesson | 9–14 | AI predicts the next word, so it can invent a book that doesn't exist. Perfect "hallucination" demo. |
| [MiFuture: AI Hallucination Lesson](https://www.mifuture.co.uk/untitled/) | Interactive lesson | 12–14 | Pattern vs truth; one early mistake spreads through a whole chat. |
| [KidsAiTools: Understanding AI hallucinations (kids)](https://www.kidsaitools.com/en/articles/understanding-ai-hallucinations-kids) | Article | 6–11, parents | "Three-check" rule: does it make sense, can I check it, what's missing. Commercial site (sells prompts), so use the idea, don't cite it as authority. |

## Gaps / notes
- No strong, current **video** found on "what not to share with AI" made for kids. Articles cover it well; this is a content gap you can fill yourself.
- Crash Course AI is from 2019: good for "how it works", outdated for chatbots.
- Google/OpenAI/Anthropic prompting guides are written for adults at work. Adapt examples to homework, drawing, games.
- Skipped as unreliable: Mobicip, FlashGet Kids, spammy video mirror sites.
- Anthropic docs URL was not returned by search directly; it's the standard docs path. Verify it opens before citing.
