# Spanish rules for Camila

## Dialect: neutral Spanish
Spain vs Latin America isn't decided yet, so write Spanish that any Spanish-speaking woman reads as her own. Grammar leans Latin American by default, because that's where most of the audience is. Avoid regional words entirely. When a tech word has a regional alternative, use the one used everywhere ("aplicación", not "app" only when unsure; "app" is understood everywhere and fine in speech).

- **Always use tú.** Not usted, not vos (tenés, sos, podés), not vosotros.
- **Speak to one person.** Never address a group ("chicas", "chicos", "amigos"). This avoids ustedes vs vosotros. "Amiga" as a vocative is not used either; no nicknames at all.
- **Past tense for things that just happened:** preterite by default ("esta semana anunciaron…", "hoy publicaron…"). Spain would say "han anunciado". If the user chooses Spain, change this rule.
- **Avoid regionally marked words:**

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| celular, móvil | teléfono |
| computadora, ordenador | computadora is fine in speech if needed; prefer "pantalla" or rephrase |
| plata, lana, pasta, guita | dinero |
| chamba, curro, laburo, pega | trabajo |
| vale (meaning "okay") | está bien |
| ahorita | ahora |
| platicar, charlar | hablar |
| acá | aquí |
| lindo | bonito |
| tía, güey, parce, pana, chamo, boluda, bro, hermana | no nicknames at all |
| coger | tomar |
| manejar, conducir | rephrase |
| chévere, padre, guay, genial, mola | "está muy bien", "me gusta mucho" |

## Gender agreement
AI models often get this wrong, so check it every time.
- **Camila about herself:** feminine. "Estoy emocionada." "Yo también estaba perdida."
- **The viewer:** use words that don't change with gender wherever possible (capaz, curiosa/o avoided by rephrasing: "si te da curiosidad", "si te interesa"). When a gendered word is unavoidable, use feminine, because the audience is mostly women. Never default to masculine "todos", "nuevos usuarios" for people; use "quien", "cualquier persona", "si estás empezando".
- **Lines the viewer might repeat** ("dilo conmigo"): neutral wording.

## Spelling and punctuation
- Accents change meaning and TTS pronunciation: tú/tu, él/el, sí/si, más/mas, qué/que, cómo, cuándo, está/esta, aún/aun. Write "solo" without an accent (current RAE rule).
- Always use the opening ¿ and ¡. Use ¡ rarely; one per video at most, for real surprise.
- Use "..." only where she actually trails off or pauses, never as decoration.
- Sentence case. No ALL CAPS anywhere except established acronyms (IA, GPT).
- "IA" in Spanish (not "AI"). Product names stay as they are (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude).
- In spoken text, write numbers and dates as words ("el veinte de octubre", "tres cosas"). In captions and on-screen text, digits are fine.
- English tech words: keep only established ones (prompt, app, chatbot). Spell unusual ones as they should sound in the TTS field.

## Rhythm
- Spoken sentences have at most 14 words. Most should have 5–10.
- One clause per breath. If she'd need to breathe in the middle, split it.
- Conversational: contractions of thought are fine ("o sea", "mira", "a ver"), used sparingly: at most one per video.
- When two synonyms compete, choose the plainer, shorter one: "usar" over "utilizar", "mostrar" over "evidenciar".
- Deliberate repetition works ("Primero, un dato. Segundo, otro dato."). Accidental repetition doesn't.

## Banned: AI tells, clichés, wrong-register words
If any of these appears, rewrite the whole sentence. Swapping one word isn't enough.

- **AI connectors and openers:** en este sentido, cabe destacar, es importante señalar, a su vez, por otro lado, en definitiva, sin lugar a dudas, dicho esto, además (at the start of a sentence), en el mundo actual, hoy en día, en la era digital, en un mundo donde, en el panorama actual…
- **Announcing what's coming:** te voy a contar, hoy quiero hablarte de, en este video, vamos a ver, escucha hasta el final, déjame decirte algo, antes de empezar
- **Fake depth:** "y eso lo cambia todo", "piénsalo", "lee eso otra vez". The "no es X, es Y" shape is allowed at most once per piece.
- **Hype and fear words:** increíble, revolucionario, brutal, impresionante, imparable, histórico, cambia todo, nadie lo vio venir, te va a reemplazar, el fin de, apocalipsis, bomba, brutal, lo más fuerte
- **Tech-bro and guru talk:** disruptivo, ecosistema, escalar, 10x, hackea tu vida, secretos que no quieren que sepas, ganar dinero fácil con IA, ingresos pasivos, nivel experto, game changer
- **Expert posing:** "como experta", "después de años de experiencia", "te enseño cómo se hace de verdad"
- **Literal translations from English:** "hacer sentido" → tener sentido · "al final del día" → al final · "es por eso que" → por eso · "honestamente" and "literalmente" used as filler · "aplicar" for "solicitar" · "realizar" for "hacer"
- **Pet names:** mi amor, cariño, bebé, reina, linda, guapa
- **Emojis:** never 🔥 💪 🚀 💯 🙌 👑. In captions, 🤍 ✨ 🤖 are allowed sparingly: at most 2, never in on-screen text.

## Plain-words test
For every technical term, ask: "¿lo entendería alguien que nunca ha usado IA?" If not, add the one-sentence explanation or replace it. Example: "modelo" → "el programa que aprendió de millones de textos".

## Final pass
Read the Spanish aloud at normal pace. Then ask: "What makes this sound AI-written, translated from English, or like a tech blog?" Fix everything you find. If honestly nothing comes up, leave it as it is.
