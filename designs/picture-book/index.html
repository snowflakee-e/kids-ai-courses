What happens in one lesson

The same five steps for every age, from a picture game at 6 to a real chatbot at 15. Then the lesson stops, on purpose.

  1. 1

    A riddle

    A question from your child's world: why can AI draw a cat but not a hand?

  2. 2

    A guess

    “What will the AI do?” Your child makes a bet before anything happens.

  3. 3

    A try

    A short task with Bloop: sort cards, train a model, fix a prompt.

  4. 4

    A surprise

    The result is not what they guessed. Bloop helps them work out why.

  5. 5

    Something of their own

    A picture, a bot, a game or a story, signed with who did what.

The end of the page. After a lesson Bloop “recharges until tomorrow”. No endless videos, no streak that burns, nothing lost for skipping a day.

A note for parents

You pay, your child plays. So the rules are written for you.

We always

  • Keep children under 13 in Bloop's own sandbox, never on public chatbots.
  • Have Bloop say it is a program, at the start of every chat.
  • Refuse names, addresses, schools and photos. Bloop hides them and explains why.
  • Send you a short weekly note: what we did and one question for dinner.
  • Let you set the daily limit. After it, the next lesson waits until tomorrow.

We never

  • Use streaks that burn or a sad mascot who “misses you”.
  • Sell loot boxes, lives or random prizes.
  • Autoplay the next video.
  • Send notifications to your child, or to anyone at night.
  • Rank kids under 13 on public leaderboards.

Questions parents ask

My child is 6. Isn't that too early?

At 6 most of the course is off-screen: sorting cards, playing “I am a robot”, short cartoons with Bloop. A grown-up runs the AI tools. Kids learn three rules and that AI is a program, not a friend.

Will my child talk to ChatGPT?

Not before 13. Younger kids use Bloop's sandbox with filters and message limits. From 13, real tools only with your consent and in teen modes.

Won't this make them use AI for homework?

We teach the opposite: AI as a coach that asks questions, not an author that writes the essay. Kids learn how to say where AI helped.

How do I know it's working?

A weekly note with your child's project, a family task for ten minutes, and a demo day at the end of each module where your child shows what they made.
