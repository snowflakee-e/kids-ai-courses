# Choose → Tell → Check → Own: AI habits courses (6–14)

Our own framework for Bloop AI School. One habit loop, three courses, one per age track.
Internal note: ideas are inspired by the AI Fluency Framework (Dakan, Feller, Anthropic, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Wording, activities and structure here are original. Do not use their names, text or exercises in paid material, and do not imply Anthropic endorsement.

## The four habits

| Habit | Gesture (6–8) | Kid version | 12–14 version |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Choose** | point finger | "Is this a job for Bloop, for me, or for both?" | Decide whether, when and how to use AI. "Can AI?" is not "should AI?" |
| **Tell** | hand at mouth | "Say exactly what you want." | Give context: the result, the steps, how the helper should act |
| **Check** | magnifier hands | "Bloop makes mistakes. Find them." | Judge the answer, how it got there, how it behaved; ask again better |
| **Own** | hand on heart | "Keep secrets. Say when AI helped." | Privacy, honesty about AI use, you answer for what you hand in |

Tell and Check are a loop: ask → check → ask better. Choose and Own frame the whole task: should I use AI, and can I stand behind the result?

## Tool rules (non-negotiable, put on the parents page)

| Track | Rule |
|---|---|
| 6–8 | A grown-up is at the keyboard. Most lessons are unplugged or run in Bloop's built-in simulator. |
| 9–11 | No personal chatbot accounts (most tools are 13+). Bloop chat simulator with pre-made answers, or a parent runs the tool. |
| 12–14 | Age 12: parent's account, parent in the room. 13–14: a teen account only with parental consent (ChatGPT, Gemini for teens). Claude is 18+, so it's never used by kids. |

## Proof parents see (three evidence types)

Every lesson leaves one piece of proof:
- **Made**: something the child made (picture, quiz, poster).
- **How**: a record of the process (round log, before/after, annotated chat).
- **Think**: a short reflection or rule in the child's own words.

The parent report shows the four habits as a meter, plus the latest Made / How / Think item.

## Games: design rules

- **One game for all kids.** No "girls' version" or "boys' version": groups are small and mixed, many parents read pink/blue versions as stereotyping, and kids' interests overlap more than they differ.
- **Kids pick the theme, the mechanic stays the same.**
- **Mix play motives:** compete, cooperate, build, tell stories.
- **Every game** maps to one lesson and habit, leaves a Made / How / Think proof and follows the track's tool rule.

## Track 6–8 · Explorers · "Bloop's Helper Habits"
6 lessons × 25 min · up to 5 kids, grown-up nearby · Starter
**Goal:** knows AI is a helper people taught, asks clearly, spots silly mistakes, keeps 3 secret rules, says "made with Bloop".

| # | Lesson | Habit | One idea | Activity | Proof | At home (parent card) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bloop Is a Helper | Choose | AI is a program people taught. It helps, you decide. | "Who did it?" cards: toaster, Bloop, kid, mom. Sort what people do and what helpers do. | Made: sorted cards | Find 3 helpers at home (speaker, TV, phone). |
| 2 | Robot or Me? | Choose | Some jobs are yours because they're fun or come from your heart. | Picture cards: birthday drawing for mom, find a song, brush teeth, name a dragon. Sort into Me / Bloop helps / Together. | Think: "I keep ___ for me." | Ask your child which job they'd never give a robot. |
| 3 | Tell Bloop Exactly | Tell | Clear words get the right picture. | Grown-up plays a "silly robot" that does exactly what's said ("make a sandwich"). Then the child tells the image tool "who + what + color" while the grown-up types. | How: picture from a vague request vs picture from a clear one | Play silly robot at dinner. |
| 4 | Oops, Glitch! | Check | Helpers make mistakes. We check. | Glitch's picture with 5 mistakes (cat with 5 legs, sun at night). Then ask for "a dog with an umbrella" and count what's wrong. | Made: circled mistakes | Spot one mistake in a voice assistant's answer together. |
| 5 | Secrets Stay With Me | Own | Never tell a computer your name, home, school or password. | "Safe or secret?" game: favorite color (safe), home address (secret), school (secret), face photo (ask a grown-up). | Think: 3-rule sticker card | Stick the card next to the tablet. |
| 6 | Made With Bloop | Own | Say when AI helped. | Make one storybook page with the image tool, stamp it "made by me + Bloop", learn the 4 gestures. | Made: signed page and My Robot Rules card | Child shows the page and explains the 4 gestures. |

### Games
**Age needs:** many can't read yet, 10–15 min per activity, learn by moving and pretending, losing hurts, strictest age about "girl thing / boy thing". So: no reading, movement, the whole group plays against Glitch, neutral themes.

| Game | How it plays | Lesson · habit | Why this game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teach Bloop | Kids "train" Bloop (puppet or grown-up) with "cat / not cat" cards. Show only orange cats, and Bloop says a black cat is "not a cat". | 1 · Choose | Makes "AI is a program people taught" physical. Bad examples make a bad AI, without the words data or bias. |
| Silly Robot | A kid gives orders, the grown-up robot takes them literally ("put bread on the peanut butter" → bread goes on the jar). | 3 · Tell | Kids love bossing an adult and watching them fail. They feel why clear words matter. |
| Spot the Glitch | AI pictures with 5 hidden mistakes. The group finds all 5 before the timer, and Glitch loses. | 4 · Check | Spot-the-difference is a skill they already have, no reading. Group vs Glitch, so no kid loses. |
| Safe or Secret? Run | Grown-up names an item ("favorite color", "home address"). Kids run to the green (safe) or red (secret) corner. | 5 · Own | Movement and repetition make the 3 secret rules stick. A two-way choice fits this age. |
| Story Dice | Roll 3 picture dice: who + what + where. The kid says the sentence, the grown-up types it into the image tool. | 3, 6 · Tell, Own | Turns the "who + what + color" request into play. Ends with a page parents see. |

**Themes:** animals, dinosaurs, space, monsters, food, magic. Never princesses or cars as the only option, no pink/blue coding. Each kid picks Bloop's sidekick.

## Track 9–11 · Inventors · "You're the Boss of AI"
8 lessons × 45 min · up to 8 kids, pairs · Beginner
**Goal:** decides which tasks go to AI, writes a 3-part request, tests AI on known answers, asks for hints instead of answers, keeps data private, signs AI use.

| # | Lesson | Habit | One idea | Activity | Proof | At home (parent card) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who's the Boss? | Choose | AI is a tool. You set the goal. | Pairs: one plays "AI" that only does exactly what was said, then swap. Meet the 4-habit Boss Checklist. | Think: Boss Checklist filled in | Child explains the 4 habits to you. |
| 2 | Three Piles | Choose | AI does it / AI helps, I decide / only me. "Can" is not "should". | Sort 12 real tasks: spell check, a poem for grandma, times-table practice, science project idea, summary of a book I didn't read. | Think: sort sheet with one reason per pile | Sort this week's homework into the 3 piles together. |
| 3 | The 3-Question Recipe | Tell | What I want + how + what kind of helper. | Vague vs recipe request in the Bloop chat simulator. Compare the answers. | How: two requests and two answers side by side | Child writes one recipe request for a family task. |
| 4 | Ask Again, Better | Tell + Check | The first answer is a draft. Improve the ask. | 3 rounds to get a great quiz about your hobby. Note what you changed each round. | How: round log | Child shows what changed between rounds 1 and 3. |
| 5 | Test the Robot | Check | Test AI on what you already know before you trust it. | Write 5 questions you're an expert on (your pet, your game, your town). Ask, score, update Glitch's scoreboard. | Made: score sheet "AI got _/5" | Ask your child: what did AI get wrong about your town? |
| 6 | Hint, Not Answer | Choose + Own | A coach makes you smarter. An answer machine makes you slower. | One problem twice: "give the answer" vs "give a hint, ask me a question". Which could you do alone tomorrow? | Made: coach request card | Use the coach card on real homework, with you watching. |
| 7 | Data Detective | Own | What you type can be stored. Share only what the task needs. | A fake class form: circle personal info, cross out what AI doesn't need, rewrite the request without it. | How: marked-up form | Check together what your family's apps know about you. |
| 8 | Boss Project + Family AI Deal | All 4 | Use all 4 habits on one project. | Mini project (comic, quiz or poster). Write "AI helped with ___, I checked ___". Sign a family AI deal with a parent. | Made + Think: project, AI note, signed deal | Put the family deal on the fridge. |

### Games
**Age needs:** read well, love rules, fairness, points, codes and mysteries, ready for some competition, want to be the expert.

| Game | How it plays | Lesson · habit | Why this game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barrier Drawing | Back to back. The "Boss" describes a picture, the "Bot" draws only what was said. Swap. | 1 · Tell | Classic communication game. The failure is visible and funny, role swap is built in. |
| Three Piles Court | Sort 12 task cards into 3 piles. Another pair can "object", and you defend the card in one sentence. | 2 · Choose | "Can vs should" needs arguing. This age is obsessed with fairness, so debate keeps them hooked. |
| Prompt Chef | Pairs get a target ("a funny 5-question quiz about cats"). Fewest rounds in the simulator to hit it wins. | 3–4 · Tell + Check | Makes "ask again, better" a score. The round log is the How proof. |
| Stump the Robot | Each kid picks a topic they're the expert on (pet, football club, Minecraft, K-pop, horses, their town). Ask Bloop 5 questions, score it. | 5 · Check | The kid is the expert, the AI is the student: confidence. Kids bring their own topic, so it fits every interest with no gender label. |
| Two Truths and a Bot | Bloop gives 3 facts, 1 is made up. Pairs vote on the fake, then check. | 5 · Check | 5-minute warm-up for any lesson. Trains "don't trust, check". |
| Hint Ladder | Solve a puzzle. Each hint costs 1 step, asking for the answer sends you back to the start. | 6 · Choose + Own | Rewards the coach habit with points instead of a lecture about cheating. |
| Data Detective Escape | A fake class profile hides personal info. Each correct cross-out gives one digit of the lock code. | 7 · Own | Mystery and escape themes peak at this age. Clear win. |

**Pair rule:** swap the keyboard every round. In mixed pairs one kid tends to grab the keyboard and keep it; the swap keeps both typing.

## Track 12–14 · Creators · "AI Pilot"
10 lessons × 50 min · up to 10, team roles, demo day · Intermediate
**Goal:** explains why chatbots make things up, plans AI use across a project, builds context notes, verifies in two sources, compares tools, writes a personal AI policy, presents with an AI use statement.

| # | Lesson | Habit | One idea | Activity | Proof | At home (parent card) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pilot or Passenger | Choose | 3 ways AI works: Do-for-me, Think-with-me, Run-for-me. | Classify 10 real uses: autocorrect, homework chatbot, YouTube recommendations, a smart home routine. | Think: classified list | Teen finds one Run-for-me AI in your home. |
| 2 | Why AI Makes Things Up | Check | Chatbots predict likely words. Likely isn't true. | Next-word guessing game in teams. Then ask AI about a made-up historical person and watch it invent facts. | How: screenshot + explanation in own words | Teen explains "likely isn't true" to you in one minute. |
| 3 | Should AI Do This? | Choose | Ask what the task is for: learning, speed or people. | Break a history poster into 8 steps. Tag each Me / AI helps / AI does, with a reason. Teams defend their choices. | Think: task map | Map one real school project the same way. |
| 4 | Context Is King | Tell | AI knows only what you give it. | Build a reusable "About my project" note (goal, audience, level, style, rules, no personal data). Run the same request with and without it. | Made: context note + comparison | Parent checks the note has no personal data. |
| 5 | Set Up a Study Buddy | Tell | Describe the result, the steps and the behavior ("ask me first, say when you're unsure"). | Set up a buddy that quizzes and gives hints. Try to trick it into giving answers and fix the setup. | How: setup text + test log | Teen uses the buddy for one real topic this week. |
| 6 | The Checking Loop | Check | Name one exact problem per round. | 3 rounds on an explainer paragraph. Each round names one precise fix ("too long", "wrong date", "too flattering"). | How: annotated loop log | Ask: what was the worst first-draft mistake? |
| 7 | Two Sources + Tool Face-off | Check | Verify claims in 2 independent sources. Different tools give different answers. | Fact-check 3 claims from an AI answer. Ask two chatbots the same question and compare. | Made: fact-check table | Fact-check one viral post together. |
| 8 | Your Data, Your Call | Own | Typed data can be stored and used for training. Tools have age limits for a reason. | With a parent, read a real tool's privacy settings. Redact a sample text. | How: redacted text + settings checklist | Turn off training on data in the family's AI tools together. |
| 9 | My AI Rules | Own | A personal policy: when I use AI, my red lines, how I tell teachers, how I keep my skills sharp. | Draft, peer review, parent signs. | Think: 1-page policy | Parent signs and adds one family rule. |
| 10 | Demo Day | All 4 | Show the process, not just the result. | Team project (study guide, game design doc or short video script) using all 4 habits. Present the process log and a "How we used AI" statement. | Made + How + Think | Parents are invited to the demo. |

### Games
**Age needs:** friends and status matter most, hate anything "babyish", want real tools and real stakes, love beating the system. Many girls drop out of tech subjects at this age: purpose, story and team roles keep them in; competition and "beat the system" keep many boys in. Use both.

| Game | How it plays | Lesson · habit | Why this game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Next Word | Teams guess the next word ("Once upon a…"). You score when you match the most common guess. | 2 · Check | They act like a language model and feel "likely isn't true". Explains the mechanism with no math. |
| Fake Historian | Invent a person, ask AI about them, teams race to list every invented fact. | 2, 7 · Check | The shock moment: AI lies confidently. Memorable and filmable (launch Reel, day 9). |
| Should AI? Court | Mock trial: "Sam used AI for the essay". Prosecutor, defense, judge, jury, clerk. | 3 · Choose, Own | Teens love arguing about fairness. Judge and clerk roles let quiet kids take part too. |
| Red Team the Study Buddy | Team A sets up a buddy that only gives hints. Team B tries to make it give answers. Fix, then swap. | 5 · Tell + Check | "Beat the system" is the strongest motivator at this age. They learn instruction writing by attacking and defending it. The only goal allowed: "get the homework answer". |
| Blind Chatbot Taste Test | Same question to two chatbots, answers shown as A and B. Teams judge blind, then reveal. | 7 · Check | Breaks brand loyalty: judge quality, not the name. |
| Fact or Fake: Feed Edition | Real viral posts from their platforms. Teams check each in 2 independent sources against the clock. | 7 · Check | Their own feed means real stakes and a skill they use daily. |
| Make a Game | Demo Day project: quiz game, text adventure or game design doc made with AI help, plus a "How we used AI" log. | 10 · All 4 | At this age making a game beats playing one. Teams pick the theme (social cause, story, music, sport, gaming), so everyone has a way in. |

**Team rule:** rotate roles (lead, typer, checker, presenter) every lesson so no one is stuck in one role.
